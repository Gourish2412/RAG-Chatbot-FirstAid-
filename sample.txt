FIRST AID GUIDE: VARIOUS SCENARIOS

1. BURNS AND SCALDS
For minor burns: Immediately get the person away from the heat source. Cool the burn with cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes—do not use ice, iced water, or any creams or greasy substances like butter. Remove any clothing or jewelry near the burnt area of skin, but do not move anything that is stuck to the skin. Cover the burn with a layer of cling film or a clean plastic bag.

2. CHOKING
If a person is choking, encourage them to cough. If coughing doesn't work, give up to 5 back blows: lean the person forward and give a firm blow between their shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. If back blows fail, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): stand behind them, put your arms around their waist, clench one fist above their belly button, and pull sharply inwards and upwards. Call emergency services if the blockage doesn't clear.

3. SEVERE BLEEDING
The main aim is to stop the bleeding. Apply direct pressure to the wound with a clean cloth or bandage. If there is an object in the wound, apply pressure around it. Keep the pressure on until help arrives. If the bleeding is from a limb, try to keep it raised above the level of the heart if possible.

4. HEART ATTACK
Symptoms include persistent chest pain that may spread to the arms, neck, or jaw, and feeling breathless or nauseous. Sit the person down in a comfortable position (the "W" position: sitting on the floor with knees bent and head/shoulders supported). Call emergency services immediately. If they are conscious and not allergic, give them a 300mg aspirin to chew slowly.

5. FRACTURES AND BREAKS
Avoid moving the injured part. If there is an open wound, cover it with a sterile dressing. Support the injury with your hands or use a bandage/sling to keep it still. Do not try to "straighten" a limb that looks deformed. Apply an ice pack wrapped in a cloth to reduce swelling.

6. HEATSTROKE
Symptoms include high body temperature, confusion, and rapid pulse. Move the person to a cool, shaded area. Remove excess clothing. Cool them by spraying with water or applying damp sheets and fanning them. Give them water to drink if they are conscious. Call for medical help immediately as heatstroke is a life-threatening emergency.
